# Pelona Hills Testimonial Request

Story arc to aim for: **Before → Concern → Decision → Experience → John's specific help → Outcome → Recommendation**

---

## 1. Email to Buyer

**Subject:** A small favor (only if you're up for it) 🏡

Hi [First Name],

Congratulations again on your new home at Pelona Hills! I hope the boxes are mostly unpacked and it's already starting to feel like home.

Thank you for trusting me with your financing. Helping families get the keys to their home is honestly one of the most rewarding parts of what I do, and I really enjoyed working with you.

I wanted to ask a small favor, and please feel free to say no. Would you be willing to record a short video about your experience?

Here's why I'm asking. Many people who are thinking about buying at Pelona Hills have the same questions and worries you may have had at the start. Hearing from someone who's actually been through it, in their own words, helps them more than anything I could tell them.

There's nothing to prepare, no script and no speech. Just talk the way you would if a friend asked, "So how did buying your house go?" Your phone is perfect, and about 60–120 seconds is plenty (more is fine if you feel like it). If you can film inside or in front of your new home, that's a great bonus, but it's not required.

Here are a few questions to get you going. Answer whichever ones you like:

1. Before you found Pelona Hills, what was your home search like, and what were you hoping to find?
2. What was your biggest concern about buying the home or getting the financing?
3. Was there a point when you realized, "Okay, we can actually make this work"? What happened?
4. What did I do during the financing process that helped you the most?
5. Was there anything about the mortgage process that surprised you, in a good way?
6. How did it feel when everything was approved and you got the keys?
7. If another buyer were thinking about Pelona Hills and working with me, what would you tell them?

It's fine to pause, laugh, restart, or go off on a tangent. Real stories are much more helpful than polished ones. If you'd rather just chat for 10 minutes and let me record the call, I'm happy to do that too.

You can text the video to me at [phone] or upload it here: [link]. Before I share anything publicly, I'll send you a short permission form so you know exactly how it would be used. Nothing goes out without your okay.

Thanks again, and welcome home!

John
[Title | Company | NMLS #]
[Phone]

## 2. Video Questions (full version, 5–7 questions)

1. **Before:** "Before you found Pelona Hills, what was your home search like, and what were you hoping to find?"
2. **Concern:** "What was your biggest concern about buying the home or getting the financing?"
3. **Turning point:** "Was there a point when you realized, 'Okay, we can actually make this work'? What happened?"
4. **John's role:** "What did John specifically do during the financing process that helped you the most?"
5. **Result:** "Was there anything about the mortgage process or financing that surprised you, in a good way?"
6. **Emotion:** "How did it feel when everything was finally approved and you got the keys to your new home?"
7. **Recommendation:** "If another buyer were considering Pelona Hills and working with John for their financing, what would you tell them?"

## 3. Easy 60-Second Version

For buyers who want to keep it really short, just 3 questions:

1. "What were you most worried about before buying your home?"
2. "What did John do that made the biggest difference?"
3. "What would you tell a friend who's thinking about buying at Pelona Hills?"

*(These three still cover concern → specific help → recommendation, so even a short clip tells a complete story.)*

## 4. Interviewer Follow-Ups

If you're doing a live interview, use **one** short follow-up whenever an answer sounds generic. Then stop and let them talk.

| If they say… | Ask… |
|---|---|
| "John was really helpful." / "He communicated well." | **"Can you give me an example?"** |
| "It was easy." / "Everything went smoothly." | **"What part were you expecting to be hard, and what happened instead?"** |
| "He saved us money." / "It worked out better than we thought." | **"What changed? The payment, cash needed, rate, or something else?"** |
| "We weren't sure we could buy." | **"What was making you think that?"** |
| "He went above and beyond." / "I'd definitely recommend him." | **"What's one moment that made you feel that way?"** |

**Interviewer guardrails**
- Never suggest an answer, number, or outcome. If they say "we saved money," let them name the figure, or leave it vague if they don't.
- Leave silence after a question. The most specific details usually come in the second sentence.
- If they ask "What should I say?", answer: "Whatever actually happened. There's no wrong answer."

## 5. Consent Reminder

⚠️ **Before you use any video publicly** (website, social media, ads, email marketing, presentations, the builder's sales office, etc.):

- [ ] **Get written permission** with a signed testimonial/media release. It should state where and how the video, the buyer's name, likeness, voice, and home may be used, for how long, and how they can revoke permission for future use.
- [ ] **Confirm everyone on camera signed**, including co-borrowers and any adults who appear. Get a parent or guardian signature for any minors, or leave kids off camera.
- [ ] **Run it through compliance** at your company before publishing. Mortgage advertising rules apply to testimonials too. Specific rate, payment, or down-payment figures mentioned by the buyer may trigger required disclosures, and your NMLS ID and Equal Housing Opportunity language need to appear.
- [ ] **Keep it honest in the edit.** Trim for length, but never splice clips so they suggest something the buyer didn't say. Only feature results that are typical, or add a clear note that results vary.
- [ ] **Disclose any incentive.** If you offer a thank-you gift, it must not depend on the review being positive, and the connection may need to be disclosed under FTC endorsement guidelines.
- [ ] **Protect privacy.** Don't show the street address, house number, loan documents, or other personal financial details unless the buyer specifically agrees.
- [ ] **Store the signed release** with the video file so you can find it later.
